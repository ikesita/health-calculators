Equations (W = kg, H = cm, A = years) — Mifflin-St Jeor: men 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5; women 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161. Harris-Benedict revised: men 88.362 + 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A; women 447.593 + 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A. Katch-McArdle: 370 + 21.6 × fat-free mass (kg), the same for both sexes. Even the best equation is typically within about ±10% of a measured BMR; muscle mass, thyroid status, illness and some medications shift the real value. BMR is a floor, not a calorie target — eating below it long-term is not recommended without medical supervision.

Common questions

BMR or TDEE — which should I use?
Use BMR to understand your resting floor. Use TDEE (BMR × activity level) for maintenance calories and for setting weight-loss or weight-gain targets — our TDEE Calculator does that step.

Why does Katch-McArdle need body fat?
Because it is built on fat-free mass: two people of the same weight burn differently at rest if one carries much more muscle. If your body fat estimate is rough, Mifflin-St Jeor is the safer default.

Does BMR change with age?
Yes — mostly because fat-free mass tends to fall with age. The equations capture this only crudely through the age term. This page gives general estimates only and is not medical advice.
