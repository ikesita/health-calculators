FFMI = fat-free mass (kg) ÷ height (m)². Adjusted FFMI = FFMI + 6.1 × (1.8 − height in m), after Kouri et al. (Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine 1995;5:223–228). *In that study of 157 male athletes, no non-user of anabolic steroids exceeded ~25, and the authors suggested ~25–26 as a practical natural ceiling for men; for women the corresponding reported ceiling is about 22, so treat scores above that with the same caution. The result is only as accurate as your body fat input — a ±3% body fat error moves FFMI by roughly ±0.7.

Common questions

How is FFMI different from BMI?
BMI uses total weight, so a muscular person can be classed "overweight". FFMI first removes fat mass, so it tracks muscularity rather than size.

What body fat number should I enter?
The most accurate you have: DEXA first, then a careful tape-measure or skinfold estimate, then a smart scale. Our Body Fat Calculator gives a Navy-method estimate if you need one.

Is a higher FFMI always better?
Not necessarily. FFMI says nothing about health markers, strength or how the mass was gained, and chasing the ceiling is not a health goal. This page is general information, not medical advice.
