Boundary values from research by Ashwell & Hsieh (2005) and Ashwell, Gunn & Gibson (2012): the 0.5 cut-off works broadly across sexes and ethnic groups, including children over 5. Measure your waist at the midpoint between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, at the end of a normal breath out. WHtR reflects central (abdominal) fat, which matters independently of BMI. This is a general estimate, not medical advice.

Common questions

What is a healthy waist-to-height ratio?
0.40–0.49. At 0.50 the risk associated with central fat starts to rise; at 0.60 it is high.

How is this different from waist-to-hip ratio?
Waist-to-hip compares waist with hips and uses sex-specific cut-offs. Waist-to-height uses one boundary — 0.5 — for almost everyone.

Where exactly do I measure my waist?
Midway between the bottom of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, tape snug but not digging in, after breathing out normally.
