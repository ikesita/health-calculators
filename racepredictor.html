Formula: T2 = T1 × (D2/D1)1.06, from Peter Riegel, "Athletic Records and Human Endurance", American Scientist 69(3), 1981 (first published 1977). Unlike a constant-pace projection, the 1.06 exponent accounts for the normal slowdown over longer distances. Predictions assume similar fitness, terrain and conditions, and work best between distances that are not too far apart — treat a 5K-to-marathon prediction as a rough guide only.

Common questions

What is the Riegel formula?
T2 = T1 × (D2 ÷ D1)1.06. The exponent 1.06 reflects the typical relationship between endurance performance at different distances.

How accurate is it?
Best when the distances are close and you trained specifically for both. It tends to be optimistic for marathons predicted from short races if your endurance training is limited.

Why not just hold the same pace?
Because nobody can. Even world-record performances slow per-kilometer as distance grows — the exponent models that slowdown.
