ACC/AHA categories (adults)
NormalSystolic < 120 AND diastolic < 80
ElevatedSystolic 120–129 AND diastolic < 80
Stage 1 hypertensionSystolic 130–139 OR diastolic 80–89
Stage 2 hypertensionSystolic ≥ 140 OR diastolic ≥ 90
Hypertensive crisisSystolic > 180 AND/OR diastolic > 120

Source: ACC/AHA Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults (2017). Your category is set by whichever number falls in the higher category. Diagnosis is based on the average of two or more readings on two or more occasions — one reading does not diagnose hypertension. Measure seated, after 5 minutes of rest, with the right cuff size, and don't talk during the measurement. This checker is a general information tool, not medical advice.

Common questions

What blood pressure is considered normal?
Systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80. Stage 1 hypertension begins at 130 systolic or 80 diastolic.

Which number matters more?
Both — the higher of the two categories wins. A reading of 118/85 is stage 1 because of the diastolic value.

Can one reading diagnose high blood pressure?
No. Guidelines use the average of at least two careful readings on at least two occasions. Home readings over several days give a much better picture.
