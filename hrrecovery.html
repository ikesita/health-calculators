1-minute heart rate recovery — general bands
12 bpm or lessBelow the clinical reference threshold*
13–25 bpmTypical range for healthy adults

Over 40 bpmExcellent — typical of trained endurance athletes

*In Cole et al. (New England Journal of Medicine 1999;341:1351–1357), a 1-minute recovery of 12 bpm or less after symptom-limited exercise was classed as abnormal and predicted higher mortality risk in that study population. These bands are general information for healthy adults, not a diagnosis. Readings are affected by how hard the effort was, whether you cool down walking or stop dead, caffeine, heat, illness and medication such as beta blockers.

Common questions

How do I measure heart rate recovery?
Note your heart rate the moment you finish exercising, then again exactly 1 minute later while walking slowly or standing. The difference is your 1-minute HRR. A chest-strap monitor gives the cleanest readings.

Can I improve my HRR?
Generally yes — regular aerobic training tends to speed up recovery over weeks to months. Track the trend with the same workout and protocol rather than chasing single readings.

Should I worry about a low HRR?
A single low reading is common after an unusually hard effort or in the heat. If your recovery is repeatedly 12 bpm or less, or you have chest pain, breathlessness or dizziness during exercise, discuss it with a doctor. This page is general information, not medical advice.
