Formula: GL = GI × available carbohydrate (g) ÷ 100. Preset GI values are typical figures from the published international tables of glycemic index (e.g. Atkinson et al., Diabetes Care 2008 and the University of Sydney database); the same food can test quite differently with ripeness, cooking and brand, so treat them as starting points. Daily totals: under ~80 is classed low, ~80–120 medium, over ~120 high. "Available carbohydrate" means total carbohydrate minus fibre where labels separate them.

Common questions

Why does watermelon come out low?
Its GI is high (~74) because its sugars absorb quickly, but a typical serving has only ~11 g of carbohydrate, so GL ≈ 8 — low. Portion is exactly what GL adds to the picture.

Does a low GL mean a food is healthy?
No. GL says nothing about fibre, micronutrients, sodium or processing. Chocolate can have a modest GL. Use GL as one lens on carbohydrate quality and quantity, not a health score.

I have diabetes. Can I rely on this?
GL can help plan meals, but individual glucose responses vary a lot and medication changes the picture. Please use this as general information only and follow your care team's guidance — it is not medical advice.
