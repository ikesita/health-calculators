Simple arithmetic: cost per day = (cigarettes per day ÷ cigarettes per pack) × pack price, using 365.25 days per year. Prices are held constant — in reality tobacco prices usually rise, so future costs are likely understated. Only the purchase price is counted; healthcare costs and lost income are not included.

Common questions

How much does a pack-a-day smoker spend in a year?
At $8 a pack: about $2,922 a year, and about $29,220 over ten years — before any price increases.

Does this include health costs?
No — only the purchase price of the cigarettes. The wider economic cost of smoking is much larger.

How much would quitting save?
The full "next 10/20/30 years" figures above. Most people who quit also report the money shows up surprisingly fast — the daily figure is the place to start.
