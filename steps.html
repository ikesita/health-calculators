Method: stride ≈ height × 0.414 (men) or × 0.413 (women) at a normal walking pace — a commonly used approximation. Calories use the rough rule of ~0.04 kcal per step for a 70 kg walker, scaled linearly by your weight. Both figures are estimates: real stride changes with pace and terrain, and calorie burn depends on speed, gradient and efficiency. Fitness trackers make the same kinds of approximations.

Common questions

Is 10,000 steps a magic number?
No — it came from a 1960s marketing campaign, not a study. Research since then finds health benefits rising from around 7,000–8,000 steps/day and levelling off somewhere in the 8,000–12,000 range for most adults. More is not harmful; the "perfect" target is the one you can sustain.

Why does my watch show a different distance?
Watches estimate stride too, and some use GPS when outdoors. Differences of 5–10% between devices are normal.

Do running steps count the same?
No. A running stride is much longer and burns roughly twice the energy per step. This converter assumes walking.
